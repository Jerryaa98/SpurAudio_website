SpurAudio A Benchmark for Studying Shortcut Learning in Few-Shot Audio Classification

Few-shot audio classifiers can be right for the wrong reason. SpurAudio mixes foreground events with controlled backgrounds, then breaks that pairing at test time to measure how much of the accuracy was a shortcut.

The shortcut

Right answer, wrong reason.

Audio is additive. A cough is never recorded alone: it is summed, in time and frequency, with the vacuum cleaner or the storm around it. When a class keeps arriving with the same background, a few-shot model can tell classes apart by their context and still look accurate.

Standard benchmarks cannot see this, because support and query share the same conditions. Move the same sound to a different room and the shortcut stops working, even though nothing about the sound itself has changed.

The benchmark

Built to pull signal and context apart.

SpurAudio draws clips from five public datasets, pairs every foreground class with four unrelated backgrounds, mixes them at a realistic level, and keeps only the mixtures that human annotators judged clean.

      The protocol

      Same episode. Swap the background.

      Evaluation uses the standard N-way K-shot episode: a few labelled support clips per class, then unlabelled queries to classify. SpurAudio samples every test episode two ways and reports the difference.

      IID

      Queries share the background pairings of their class's support clips. This is what a standard benchmark measures.

      OOD

      Queries arrive with backgrounds that also appear next to other classes. N, K and the number of queries stay the same.

      Δ

      Δ = AccIID − AccOOD
      The share of accuracy that depended on the background.
